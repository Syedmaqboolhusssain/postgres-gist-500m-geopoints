======================================================================
 3. Two points 100 m apart can have nothing in common
======================================================================
A geohash cell is a grid square, so proximity in space does not imply a
shared prefix -- a point just west of a cell edge and one just east of it
may diverge at the very first character. 1000 random pairs, each exactly
one pair 100 m apart, sampled over the NYC area:
WITH a AS (
  SELECT ST_SetSRID(ST_MakePoint(-74.0 + random()*0.5, 40.6 + random()*0.4), 4326) AS p
  FROM generate_series(1, 1000)
), pairs AS (
  SELECT p,
         ST_SetSRID(ST_MakePoint(ST_X(p) + 0.0012, ST_Y(p)), 4326) AS q  -- ~100 m east
  FROM a
), gh AS (
  -- The geography cast is what makes "100 m" a real number. In the built-in
  -- point type 0.0012 degrees of longitude is not a distance at all; it is
  -- ~101 m at New York's latitude and ~133 m at the equator.
  SELECT ST_Distance(p::geography, q::geography) AS metres,
         ST_GeoHash(p, 7) AS gp, ST_GeoHash(q, 7) AS gq
  FROM pairs
)
SELECT count(*)                                                   AS pairs_tested,
       round(avg(metres)::numeric, 1)                             AS avg_metres_apart,
       count(*) FILTER (WHERE gp <> gq)                           AS in_different_cells,
       count(*) FILTER (WHERE left(gp,5) <> left(gq,5))           AS differ_by_char_5,
       count(*) FILTER (WHERE left(gp,3) <> left(gq,3))           AS differ_by_char_3
FROM gh;
 pairs_tested | avg_metres_apart | in_different_cells | differ_by_char_5 | differ_by_char_3 
--------------+------------------+--------------------+------------------+------------------
         1000 |            101.3 |                877 |               32 |                0
(1 row)

Time: 55.233 ms

This is why a correct geohash "nearby" search must query the cell PLUS its
eight neighbours and then re-check distances -- nine range scans and a
post-filter to answer what GiST answers with one operator. GiST's bounding
boxes overlap and adapt to the data, so it has no equivalent of an edge.
